### What is the Standard Normal Distribution?

You already know what a normal distribution looks like — a bell-shaped curve.

Different datasets have different means and standard deviations:

- Student marks: Mean = 70, SD = 10  
- Heights: Mean = 170, SD = 20  
- Salaries: Mean = 500, SD = 100  

All of them have the same **bell shape**, but they are centered at different places.

**Problem**:  
If every dataset has a different mean and standard deviation, do we need a separate probability table for each one? That would be impossible.

---

### The Solution: Standard Normal Distribution

Mathematicians created a **common standard version** of the normal distribution so that we can use **one single table** (Z-table) for all normal distributions.

### Definition

**Standard Normal Distribution** is a normal distribution with:

- **Mean (μ) = 0**
- **Standard Deviation (σ) = 1**

It is denoted as **Z ~ N(0, 1)**.

---

### The Graph of Standard Normal Distribution

              /   \
            /       \

       -3  -2  -1   0   1   2   3

- Center is at **0**
- One standard deviation = **1**
- Two standard deviations = **2**
- Three standard deviations = **3**

---

### Why Do We Need It?

**Example:**

**Dataset A**: Mean = 70, SD = 10  
Question: P(X < 85)

**Dataset B**: Mean = 170, SD = 20  
Question: P(X < 200)

Instead of calculating separately, we convert both to **Z-scores**:

- For Dataset A: Z = (85 - 70) / 10 = **1.5**
- For Dataset B: Z = (200 - 170) / 20 = **1.5**

Now both questions become:  
**P(Z < 1.5)**

We only need **one Z-table** for all normal distributions in the world.

---

### Standardization

The process of converting a normal random variable **X** into a standard normal variable **Z** is called **Standardization**.

**Formula**:
**Here’s the updated version with the formula in a clean, readable text form:**

---

### Standardization Formula

**Z = (X - μ) / σ**

**Where:**
- **Z** = Z-score (Standard Score)
- **X** = Original value from the normal distribution
- **μ** (mu) = Mean of the original distribution
- **σ** (sigma) = Standard Deviation of the original distribution

### Important Observation

> Z-score and Standard Normal Distribution are not two different things.

When you calculate the Z-score, you are **transforming** your original data into the **Standard Normal Distribution**.

### Interview Questions

**Q1. Why do we convert to Standard Normal Distribution?**  
**Answer**: To convert any normal distribution into a common distribution with mean = 0 and standard deviation = 1, so that we can use a single Z-table for probability calculations.

**Q2. What is the mean of a Standard Normal Distribution?**  
**Answer**: **0**

**Q3. What is the standard deviation of a Standard Normal Distribution?**  
**Answer**: **1**

**Q4. What is standardization?**  
**Answer**: The process of converting an observation (X) from any normal distribution into its Z-score, which follows the Standard Normal Distribution.

# Deep Understanding of the Standard Normal Distribution

This is something that many people never fully understand, even after years of using statistics.

## The Biggest Question

We have learned:

**Z = (X - μ) / σ**

But...

**What actually happens after we calculate the Z-score?**

Let's understand.

### Imagine this distribution
**Student Marks**  
**Mean = 70**  
**SD = 10**

Now suppose a student scored **80**.

We calculate:

**Z = (80 - 70) / 10 = 1**

### Here's the important question:

**Did the student's marks change?**

**No.**  
The student still scored 80.

### Then what changed?

**Only the scale changed.**

Instead of measuring in *marks*, we are now measuring in *standard deviations*.

**Original scale:**
Marks
30   40   50   60   70   80   90

The graph hasn't changed.  
The shape hasn't changed.  
**Only the labels on the x-axis changed.**

---

Think of temperature.

Suppose today it is **25°C**.  
In Fahrenheit, it becomes **77°F**.

Did the weather change? **No.**  
Only the unit changed.

**Exactly the same thing happens with Z-score.**

Marks become **Standard Deviations**.

This is why it's called **Standardization**.

You are converting:
- **Marks** → **Standard Deviation Units**

---

## This is the Standard Normal Distribution

**Before standardization:**
- Mean = 70
- SD = 10

**After standardization:**
- Mean = 0
- SD = 1

### Notice something amazing

**Every Normal Distribution in the world becomes:**
- Mean = 0
- SD = 1

This is why we only need **one Z-table**.

---

## Why is the mean always 0?

Let's prove it.

Suppose X = Mean (μ).  
Then:

**Z = (μ - μ) / σ = 0 / σ = 0**

So the mean always becomes **0**.

---

## Why is SD always 1?

Suppose a value is exactly one SD above the mean.

Original value: **Mean + SD**

**Z = ((μ + σ) - μ) / σ = σ / σ = 1**

- One SD above the mean becomes **1**
- Two SD above becomes **2**
- Three SD above becomes **3**

Everything gets converted into SD units.

---

## ⭐ This is the deepest understanding

A **Z-score** is not just a formula.  

It is a **change of measurement unit**.

Instead of saying:  
> "The student scored 80 marks."

We say:  
> "The student scored 1 standard deviation above the mean."

That's all.

---

## Interview Question

**Interviewer:** What is the purpose of calculating a Z-score?

**Weak Answer:**  
"To find probability." *(Not completely correct)*

**Excellent Answer:**  
The purpose of calculating a Z-score is **to standardize observations** by converting them from their original units into **standard deviation units**. This allows values from different normal distributions to be compared on a common scale and enables the use of the Standard Normal Distribution and the Z-table.
