# Confidence Interval (CI)

## What is a Confidence Interval?

A **Confidence Interval (CI)** is a range of values used to estimate an unknown population parameter, usually the population mean.

Instead of giving only one estimate, we give a range in which the true population value is likely to lie.

**Example:**

Instead of saying:

> Average height of men in India = **170 cm**

We say:

> Average height of men in India is estimated to be between **168 cm and 172 cm**.

This interval, from 168 cm to 172 cm, is called the **Confidence Interval**.

---

# Why do we need a Confidence Interval?

In real life, we usually cannot collect data from the entire population.

For example:

* We cannot measure the height of every man in India.
* We cannot ask every voter whom they will vote for.
* We cannot inspect every product manufactured in a factory.

Instead, we:

1. Take a random sample.
2. Calculate the sample mean.
3. Estimate the population mean using a confidence interval.

Since different samples produce different sample means, giving a **range** is more reliable than giving a single number.

---

# Sample Mean vs Population Mean

* **Population Mean (mu):** The actual average of the entire population, which is usually unknown.
* **Sample Mean (x-bar):** The average calculated from a sample.

Different samples give different sample means.

Example:

Sample 1 → Mean = 170 cm

Sample 2 → Mean = 171 cm

Sample 3 → Mean = 169 cm

Because of this sampling variation, we estimate the population mean using a confidence interval.

---

# Standard Error (SE)

Before calculating a confidence interval, we calculate the Standard Error.

Formula:

SE = population standard deviation divided by the square root of sample size

Where:

* sigma = population standard deviation
* n = sample size

### Interpretation

Standard Error measures how much the **sample means** vary from the true population mean.

* Larger sample size → Smaller Standard Error
* Smaller sample size → Larger Standard Error

---

# Margin of Error (ME)

The Margin of Error tells us how far the confidence interval extends on either side of the sample mean.

Formula:

ME = Z × SE

For a 95% Confidence Interval:

Z = 1.96

Therefore:

ME = 1.96 × SE

---

# Confidence Interval Formula

When the population standard deviation is known:

CI = sample mean plus or minus Z times (population standard deviation divided by the square root of sample size)

Where:

* sample mean = average from the sample
* Z = Z-score corresponding to the confidence level
* sigma = population standard deviation
* n = sample size

---

# Why is Z = 1.96?

The Standard Normal Distribution has:

* Mean = 0
* Standard Deviation = 1

For a 95% Confidence Interval, 95% of the area lies between:

-1.96 < Z < 1.96

This leaves:

* 2.5% in the left tail
* 2.5% in the right tail

Total outside area = 5%.

Therefore, the critical Z-value for a 95% Confidence Interval is **1.96**.

---

# Example 1

Given:

Sample Mean = 50

Population SD = 10

Sample Size = 25

### Step 1: Calculate Standard Error

SE = 10 divided by the square root of 25

SE = 10 divided by 5

SE = 2

### Step 2: Calculate Margin of Error

ME = 1.96 × 2

ME = 3.92

### Step 3: Calculate Confidence Interval

50 plus or minus 3.92

Lower Limit:

50 - 3.92 = 46.08

Upper Limit:

50 + 3.92 = 53.92

Final Answer:

(46.08, 53.92)

Interpretation:

We are **95% confident** that the true population mean lies between **46.08 and 53.92**.

---

# Example 2 (Effect of Sample Size)

Suppose:

Sample Mean = 50

Population SD = 10

### Case 1

Sample Size = 25

SE = 10 divided by 5 = 2

Margin of Error:

1.96 × 2 = 3.92

Confidence Interval:

(46.08, 53.92)

---

### Case 2

Sample Size = 100

SE = 10 divided by 10 = 1

Margin of Error:

1.96 × 1 = 1.96

Confidence Interval:

(48.04, 51.96)

Observation:

Increasing the sample size makes the confidence interval **narrower** because the estimate becomes more precise.

---

# What does "95% Confidence" mean?

Correct Interpretation:

If we repeatedly take random samples and construct a 95% confidence interval for each sample, then approximately **95% of those intervals will contain the true population mean**, while about **5% will not**.

Incorrect Interpretation:

❌ "There is a 95% probability that the true mean lies inside this particular interval."

Reason:

The population mean is a fixed value.

The interval changes from sample to sample.

---

# Relationship Between Sample Size and Confidence Interval

As Sample Size Increases:

* Standard Error decreases.
* Margin of Error decreases.
* Confidence Interval becomes narrower.
* Estimate becomes more precise.

As Sample Size Decreases:

* Standard Error increases.
* Margin of Error increases.
* Confidence Interval becomes wider.
* Estimate becomes less precise.

---

# Key Formulas

### Standard Error

SE = population standard deviation divided by the square root of sample size

### Margin of Error

ME = Z × SE

### Confidence Interval

CI = sample mean plus or minus Z times (population standard deviation divided by the square root of sample size)

# Interview Questions

## Q1. What is a Confidence Interval?

A Confidence Interval is a range of values used to estimate an unknown population parameter with a specified level of confidence.

---

## Q2. Why do we use Confidence Intervals?

Because the population mean is usually unknown, and different samples produce different sample means. A confidence interval provides a reasonable range for estimating the true population mean.

---

## Q3. What is the difference between Standard Deviation and Standard Error?

**Standard Deviation (SD):**

Measures the variability of individual observations.

**Standard Error (SE):**

Measures the variability of sample means.

Formula:

SE = population standard deviation divided by the square root of sample size

---

## Q4. What happens to the Confidence Interval if the sample size increases?

* Standard Error decreases.
* Margin of Error decreases.
* Confidence Interval becomes narrower.
* Estimate becomes more precise.

---

## Q5. What is the Margin of Error?

The Margin of Error is the amount added to and subtracted from the sample mean to create the confidence interval.

Formula:

ME = Z × SE

---

## Q6. Why is 1.96 used?

Because in the Standard Normal Distribution, 95% of the area lies between -1.96 and 1.96, making 1.96 the critical Z-value for a 95% confidence interval.

---


# Additional Confidence Interval Interview Questions

## Q9. What is the purpose of a Confidence Interval?

A Confidence Interval provides a range of plausible values for an unknown population parameter instead of giving only a single estimate. It also indicates the precision of our estimate.

---

## Q10. Why is a Confidence Interval preferred over a point estimate?

A point estimate provides only one value and does not indicate uncertainty.

A Confidence Interval provides both:

* an estimate
* the uncertainty associated with that estimate

Therefore, it is more informative.

---

## Q11. What happens if the confidence level increases from 95% to 99%?

The confidence interval becomes **wider**.

Reason:

A higher confidence level requires a larger critical value (Z-value), which increases the Margin of Error.

Example:

95% Confidence

Z = 1.96

99% Confidence

Z = 2.576

Since

ME = Z × SE

the Margin of Error increases.

---

## Q12. What happens if the confidence level decreases?

The confidence interval becomes **narrower** because the Margin of Error decreases.

---

## Q13. What factors affect the width of a Confidence Interval?

Three main factors affect the width:

1. Sample Size
2. Standard Deviation
3. Confidence Level

---

## Q14. How does Standard Deviation affect the Confidence Interval?

Higher Standard Deviation

→ Higher Standard Error

→ Larger Margin of Error

→ Wider Confidence Interval

Lower Standard Deviation

→ Smaller Standard Error

→ Smaller Margin of Error

→ Narrower Confidence Interval

---

## Q15. Why does increasing the sample size make the Confidence Interval narrower?

Because

SE = σ / √n

As sample size increases,

Standard Error decreases,

which decreases the Margin of Error,

making the Confidence Interval narrower.

---

## Q16. Does increasing the sample size change the population standard deviation?

No.

The population standard deviation remains the same.

Only the Standard Error changes.

---

## Q17. Does the sample mean always equal the population mean?

No.

The sample mean is an estimate of the population mean.

Different samples produce different sample means.

---

## Q18. What assumptions are required to calculate a Confidence Interval?

* The sample should be randomly selected.
* Observations should be independent.
* The population should be approximately normal, or the sample size should be sufficiently large (Central Limit Theorem).

---

## Q19. What is the difference between a Confidence Interval and a Prediction Interval?

Confidence Interval:

Estimates the population mean.

Prediction Interval:

Estimates where a future individual observation is likely to fall.

Prediction intervals are always wider than confidence intervals.

---

## Q20. Can a Confidence Interval prove that the population mean is inside the interval?

No.

A Confidence Interval cannot guarantee that the population mean lies inside the interval.

It only provides a range that would contain the true population mean in the stated percentage of repeated samples.

---

## Q21. Why do we use Standard Error instead of Standard Deviation in the Confidence Interval formula?

Because the Confidence Interval estimates the population mean using the **sample mean**.

The variability of sample means is measured by the **Standard Error**, not by the Standard Deviation.

---

## Q22. Which is more precise: a wider or a narrower Confidence Interval?

A narrower Confidence Interval is more precise because it provides a smaller range of plausible values.

---

## Q23. Can two different samples produce different Confidence Intervals?

Yes.

Different samples produce different sample means, resulting in different confidence intervals.

---

## Q24. What is the relationship between Confidence Interval and Hypothesis Testing?

They are closely related.

For example, a 95% Confidence Interval corresponds to a hypothesis test performed at a significance level of α = 0.05.

If the hypothesized population mean lies outside the 95% Confidence Interval, we reject the null hypothesis at the 5% significance level.

---

## Q25. What is the most commonly used Confidence Level?

* 90%
* 95% ✅ (Most common)
* 99%

---

# Quick Revision

Remember these formulas:

Standard Error

SE = σ / √n

Margin of Error

ME = Z × SE

Confidence Interval

CI = x̄ ± ME

# One-line Interview Summary

A Confidence Interval is a statistical range calculated from sample data that is used to estimate an unknown population parameter with a specified level of confidence.

A larger sample size produces a smaller Standard Error, resulting in a narrower and more precise Confidence Interval.

# Summary

* Confidence Interval estimates the unknown population mean.
* It is expressed as:

sample mean plus or minus Margin of Error

* Standard Error measures the variability of sample means.
* Margin of Error = Z × Standard Error.
* Larger samples produce smaller Standard Errors.
* Smaller Standard Errors produce narrower Confidence Intervals.
* A 95% Confidence Interval uses Z = 1.96.
* Confidence Intervals provide a range of plausible values for the population mean rather than a single estimate.
