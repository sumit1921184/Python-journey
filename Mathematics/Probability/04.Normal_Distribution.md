**Detailed Notes on Normal Distribution**

### 1. Introduction to Normal Distribution
The **Normal Distribution** (also called Gaussian Distribution) is one of the most important probability distributions in statistics. It is symmetric and bell-shaped, and many natural phenomena (heights, IQ scores, measurement errors, etc.) approximately follow it.

**Key properties**:
- Symmetric around the mean.
- Mean = Median = Mode.
- Defined by two parameters: **mean (μ)** and **standard deviation (σ)**.
- The total area under the curve is always **1** (100% probability).

### 2. Probability Density Function (PDF)

The PDF gives the relative likelihood of a random variable taking a particular value. For a normal distribution:

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left(-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
$$

- **μ** (mu): Location of the center (mean).
- **σ** (sigma): Controls the spread (standard deviation).

**Standard Normal Distribution** (μ = 0, σ = 1) simplifies this to:

$$
\phi(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-z^2/2}
$$



**Graph**: Standard Normal PDF (bell curve).

### 3. Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF)

The CDF gives the probability that a random variable is **less than or equal to** a certain value:

$$
F(x) = P(X \leq x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} f(t) \, dt
$$

For the standard normal, this is denoted as **Φ(z)**.



**Graph**: Standard Normal CDF (S-shaped curve that approaches 0 on the left and 1 on the right).

### 4. Mean, Variance, and Standard Deviation

- **Mean (μ)**: The center of the distribution. It shifts the bell curve left or right.
- **Variance (σ²)**: Measures the average squared deviation from the mean.
- **Standard Deviation (σ)**: Square root of variance. Tells how spread out the data is.

**Interpretation**:
- Larger σ → wider and flatter curve.
- Smaller σ → taller and narrower curve.

### 5. Z-Score (Standardization)

The **Z-score** transforms any normal random variable to the **standard normal distribution** (μ=0, σ=1):

$$
Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}
$$

- Positive Z-score → value is above the mean.
- Negative Z-score → value is below the mean.
- Z = 0 → exactly at the mean.

**Why standardize?** It allows us to use a single Z-table for all normal distributions.

### 6. 68–95–99.7 Rule (Empirical Rule)

For any normal distribution:

- **≈68%** of data falls within **1 standard deviation** of the mean (μ ± σ).
- **≈95%** falls within **2 standard deviations** (μ ± 2σ).
- **≈99.7%** falls within **3 standard deviations** (μ ± 3σ).



**Graph**: Visual representation of the Empirical Rule with shaded areas.

### 7. Reading the Z-Table

The **Z-table** (standard normal table) provides **Φ(z) = P(Z ≤ z)** for standard normal variables.

**How to read**:
1. Break Z into two parts: e.g., 1.23 → row 1.2, column 0.03.
2. The table value is the cumulative probability from -∞ to that z.
3. For negative z, use symmetry: Φ(-z) = 1 - Φ(z).

**Common values**:
- Φ(0) = 0.5000
- Φ(1) ≈ 0.8413
- Φ(2) ≈ 0.9772
- Φ(3) ≈ 0.9987

### 8. Positive and Negative Z-Scores

- **Positive Z** (right of mean): Area to the left is > 0.5.
- **Negative Z** (left of mean): Area to the left is < 0.5.
- Due to symmetry, probabilities are mirrored around the mean.

### 9. Finding Probabilities

#### a. Left-tail probability: P(Z ≤ z)
- Directly from Z-table.

#### b. Right-tail probability: P(Z > z)
- = 1 - P(Z ≤ z)

#### c. Between two values: P(a < Z < b)
- = P(Z ≤ b) - P(Z ≤ a)

**Examples** (using standard normal):

1. P(Z < 1.5) ≈ 0.9332
2. P(Z > 1.5) = 1 - 0.9332 = 0.0668
3. P(-1 < Z < 1) = Φ(1) - Φ(-1) = 0.8413 - 0.1587 = 0.6826 (≈68%)

**For any normal distribution**:
- Convert your X values to Z-scores first.
- Then use the standard normal probabilities.
